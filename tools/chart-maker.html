How to make a chart from CSV

  1. Add your data. Drop a .csv file or paste rows copied from a spreadsheet. The first row must contain column names. Commas, semicolons and tabs are detected automatically.
  2. Choose a chart type. Use bars to compare categories, lines or areas for change over time, pie or doughnut for a few parts of a whole, scatter for the relationship between two numbers, and radar for profiles across several measures. Unsure? Use the Chart Guide.
  3. Pick columns. The label column becomes the axis categories. Tick one or more numeric value columns to plot as series.
  4. Refine and export. Add a title that states the takeaway, sort bars by size, choose a palette, and download a high-resolution PNG with a white background.

Tips for clearer charts

  • Bar charts should start at zero. Ours always do, so differences aren't exaggerated.
  • Limit pies to five or six slices. More than that, a sorted bar chart reads faster.
  • Write titles as findings ("Profit doubled from January to June") rather than labels ("Profit by month").
Privacy: your data never leaves this browser tab. Charts are drawn locally with the open-source Chart.js library.
